Who wins in you?

Classical economics assumes you always listen to the angel: that you decide calmly, with full information, thinking of your future self. Behavioral economics studies why the devil wins so often.

Ten pillars of happiness

What behavioral economics, psychology and experiments have learned about a happier life, distilled into ten ideas.

  1. 01

    Health and beauty

    Our grandmothers were right: health, money and love. Without health, nothing else can be enjoyed. You can't choose your genes, but charisma can be cultivated.

    Try this Walk for 20 minutes today without your phone. In your next conversation, listen without preparing your reply.

  2. 02

    Money and finance

    Being poor is expensive, and borrowing out of impatience is the fastest way to get there. Master your impatience and compound interest starts working for you.

    Try this Before buying on credit, ask what it would cost you to wait a month.

  3. 03

    Procrastination

    If you found Aladdin's lamp, would you wish to be happy? In experiments, almost nobody does until someone reminds them. We postpone happiness because we never define it.

    Try this Write today, in one line, what being happy means to you.

  4. 04

    Flow

    Comparing ourselves with others steals our calm; mastering something gives it back. Flow, losing yourself in a demanding task, is what makes years of practice possible.

    Try this Pick a hard task and give it 25 uninterrupted minutes, with your phone in another room.

  5. 05

    Love

    We are social animals: close bonds, romantic or not, are among the strongest sources of happiness. Like any good, affection is valued best when it is neither oversupplied nor rationed.

    Try this Today, write to someone you love and haven't seen in a while, without asking for anything.

  6. 06

    Control

    Accepting what you cannot control is liberating. It is not resignation: it is putting your energy where you actually decide.

    Try this Split your worries into two columns: what depends on you and what doesn't.

  7. 07

    Trust, forgiveness and gratitude

    Distrust is costly for health and relationships. Being able to forgive and to be grateful are among the best predictors of long-run happiness.

    Try this Before bed, write down three things you are grateful for today.

  8. 08

    Purpose (ikigai)

    'Do what you love and you'll never work a day' is bad advice. Purpose appears where what you love, what you are good at, what the world needs and what you can be paid for meet.

    Try this Draw the four circles and write one thing in each. Notice what repeats.

  9. 09

    Awareness

    Willpower is a scarce resource. You can't control it directly, but you can control your breathing, and that already changes something.

    Try this Three minutes of slow breathing before an important decision.

  10. 10

    Spirituality

    The hardest question of all: what is consciousness? It is a frontier where experimental economics is starting to have something to say.

    Try this Tonight, look at the sky for five minutes and do nothing else.
